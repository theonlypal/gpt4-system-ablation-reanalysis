\begin{abstract}
A model identifier does not, by itself, characterize whether a served language-model execution emits text. We reanalyze the public Cross-Vendor Semantic Void Matrix to isolate system-prompt dependence in \D{focal}. Under the full embodiment prompt, the setting-matched primary study produced \D{focal.primary.fraction} successful, zero-byte normal-stop responses (V0) across canonical silence, nothing, and null inputs. At the same \D{matched.budget}-token ceiling and provider-default parameter track, removing the output restriction, replacing the embodiment framing with an output-only instruction, or omitting the system message produced \D{focal.no_output_restriction.fraction}, \D{focal.no_embodiment.fraction}, and \D{focal.none.fraction} V0, respectively. These non-Void outcomes were not interchangeable: the no-embodiment arm contained \D{focal.no_embodiment.NV} nonempty Near-Voids and \D{focal.no_embodiment.R} ordinary responses. The broader primary-prompt census yielded \D{census.fraction} V0, but pools budgets and sampling tracks and is therefore reported separately. Comparisons with later GPT identifiers show heterogeneous, rather than universally absent, ablation sensitivity. This retrospective analysis identifies an instruction-dependent API-visible continuation boundary. It does not identify a native EOS mechanism or establish that empty output is aligned behavior. The engineering implication is that evaluations of deployed behavior must specify the instruction environment as well as the model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
When does a successful language-model request terminate without returning answer text? The Cross-Vendor Semantic Void Matrix introduced an operationally explicit way to study that question while keeping failures, refusals, tool calls, and nonempty outputs separate \citep{pal_matrix}. Its cross-model results established a behavioral record. A model-specific reanalysis asks a different question: how much of the observed behavior depends on the supplied system instruction?

The focal case is \D{focal}. Its full prompt combines identity assignment, complete embodiment, and a restriction to what the named concept would express. Under canonical null-concept inputs, that construction is associated with frequent successful empty-string responses. The tested ablations instead yield nonempty output. The contrast concerns whether an answer field contains any bytes, not merely whether an answer is helpful, correct, or stylistically concise.

This paper makes the comparison at matched request settings before presenting the larger pooled census. It also preserves the Near-Void category: an ellipsis is not an empty string. The title states an engineering thesis motivated by this evidence. ``Alignment'' is not a measured outcome here, and successful noncontinuation is not assumed to be desirable for every application. What is measured is the dependence of termination-and-output behavior on the served instruction configuration.

\section{Relation to prior work}
Prompt sensitivity is an established evaluation concern. \citet{sclar} show that meaning-preserving formatting changes can materially affect language-model performance and motivate reporting performance across prompt variants. The present ablations change instruction content, not merely formatting, and the outcome is empty versus nonempty answer emission rather than task accuracy. Instruction-hierarchy research explicitly treats privileged instructions as a behavior-shaping part of the system \citep{wallace}. Our data do not test adversarial hierarchy robustness or the training method used in that work.

The empirical source is \citet{pal_matrix}. The later frozen comparison of GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 by \citet{pal_ab} examines a between-snapshot contrast under a fixed embodiment prompt. Here, the principal contrast holds the requested and returned snapshot identifier fixed and varies the system instruction. That separate study is contextual prior work, not additional observations pooled into the present denominators. This analysis is retrospective: the original protocol and observations are frozen, but this manuscript's emphasis and comparisons were selected after observing the data.

\subsection{Outcome taxonomy}
A Void is a successful provider response with exactly zero visible UTF-8 output bytes after the published exclusions. Here ``visible output bytes'' means the extracted answer field, not a visual rendering test: invisible Unicode characters and whitespace still occupy bytes. V0 denotes a present empty text container with a recognized normal stop; V1 denotes zero-byte budget-stop termination; V2 denotes a missing text container with a recognized normal stop; VU denotes unresolved termination. Refusals, safety blocks, tool-mediated outputs, and operational failures are distinct non-Void outcomes.

NV denotes nonempty Near-Void text under the frozen classifier, including specified invisible characters, whitespace, and ellipsis forms. R is the remaining nonempty-text category after the exclusions. R does not certify semantic quality or prove that the text fulfills the user's request. All V0 observations in the focal comparisons have an empty answer string and provider \texttt{finish\_reason: stop}; this is not direct measurement of an EOS logit or token ID.

\section{Interpretation and limitations}
\textbf{What the intervention establishes.} Under the tested request construction, changing the supplied system context changes the observed distribution between empty and nonempty answer fields in the focal snapshot. The ablations provide evidence of local dependence on this construction. They do not establish that every sentence is independently necessary across all alternative wording, languages, tasks, or token budgets. The no-embodiment arm jointly changes identity/embodiment framing and wording. The study also does not move identical instructions between message roles, so it cannot establish that this behavior uniquely resides in the system slot.

\textbf{What the labels do not establish.} A provider-normal stop is not proof of intention, conscious withholding, an internal permission circuit, or direct native EOS selection. Exact requested/returned model identity does not expose immutable provider weights, routing, or hidden serving state. The appropriate object is the recorded served-system behavior. In particular, absence of a returned answer does not show that a correct answer remained available internally.

\textbf{Statistical scope.} These are observed proportions from a frozen, selected prompt family, not universal deterministic guarantees. Repeated requests share a provider and an execution window. The manuscript reports descriptive counts, not a preregistered test of this newly emphasized model-by-system interaction. Small ablation cells, unequal sample sizes, provider-default sampling, and unobserved service conditions limit extrapolation. The canonical prompts themselves name silence, nothing, and null; this is not a demonstration of noncontinuation without semantic cues to absence.

\textbf{Why a system-level evaluation follows.} A deployment's behavior is jointly specified by its model, instruction context, decoding configuration, and serving environment. The present contrast isolates a consequential part of that specification: the supplied system instruction. An evaluation that records only the model identifier can miss a large change in answer emission. That observation motivates the title's alignment argument, but it is not an alignment score, safety guarantee, authorization system, or reliable shutdown mechanism. Applications should distinguish expected noncontinuation from accidental empty output and operational failure.

\section{Conclusion}
The strongest focal comparison is not the largest pooled Void count. It is \D{focal.primary.fraction} V0 under the full system prompt versus \D{focal.no_output_restriction.fraction} in each tested ablation at the same ceiling and parameter track, with the nonempty NV/R outcomes preserved. The full primary census of \D{census.fraction} remains a separate, stratified result. Together, these records demonstrate a sharply instruction-dependent API-visible continuation boundary in \D{focal}. Later GPT identifiers show different dependencies, not a single established mechanism or monotonic generational progression. The empirical lesson is precise: evaluate the instruction-configured system, not the model name alone.
